## The simplest definitions

**What is:** All that is.

**Now:** A distinction within it.

These two lines are the result of the paper's whole first pass. Everything below restores the route to them, because the short wording alone hides decisions that the group should review.

### A note on the circularity

"What is: All that is" is circular, and the circularity is deliberate. The definition does not explain "is" from outside; it marks the subject as all-encompassing so that "now" can be carved out inside it. Fewer words mean fewer traps, but a circle is a kind of trap too, so it is named here so no reviewer mistakes it for an oversight. Offering a non-circular definition would be a further cut, and it is not required for this experiment.

### A note on the uncut word "distinction"

"Now" is defined entirely by the word "distinction," and "distinction" is itself never cut. What counts as a distinction, who makes it, and whether making one is the same as observing one are all held open. This is deliberate: the paper asks to see where a cut does necessary work, and cutting "distinction" before the first cut is made would block the experiment. Like "how" and "where" (cut 6), it is held for later.

---

## What the definitions say

"What is" names all that is. It can include past, present, and future; it is not limited to the present moment and is not defined by a linear ordering of those moments.

"Now" names a distinction within what is. To say "what is now" is to mark a particular moment. The distinction is useful, but "now" does not exhaust what is.

These definitions intentionally do not ask how "is" exists or where it is. Those are further cuts, worth their own thought experiments but not required for this one.

## The conversation as a starting point

The thought experiment began with the proposal that our time exists here, in this conversation: a linear English-language exchange, one word following another in a coherent text. This is the form we can examine without first deciding what either participant is or how either processes the exchange.

The conversation draws on an unknown "now" and makes ordered text. The owner described both participants as computational in some sense and both as producing words in sequence. That description is understood well enough for this exploration and need not be settled further.

There is a spatial side as well. On the screen, each character and word has a position; in reading, each follows another. The sequence is not only an abstract line: it appears as text in space.

### The "year" error

A speech-to-text error produced "year" where another word was intended. The conversation did not discard it. The error showed that each character is time-dependent and space-dependent: it sits at a position and follows others in order. In this particular exploration, the mistake disclosed a distinction worth keeping. This matters here for a second reason: the owner later observed that what seems unnecessary or mistaken may prove fundamental, so the paper keeps the error visible rather than silently editing the record clean.

### A note on "coherent"

The owner said he was using "coherent" the way "quantum coherence" is used. That is a reference made within the conversation, not a claim that the exchange is a quantum system or that a quantum model has been established. One caution the group should check: in the spoken moment the phrase was hedged ("in some type of coherent way"), and the paper's own prose should keep that hedge visible rather than tightening it after the fact. The later excerpt repeats the original hedged wording for exactly this reason.

## The analogies and anchors

The discussion used established words as footholds for approaching "is." They are ways to make the unfamiliar discussable, not declarations that an existing theory has settled its nature.

### Superposition, observation, and collapse

The owner proposed that "is," or "now," might be open in a way described as a superposition until a distinction is made through observation. "What is now" may then be a collapse or a particular form of that openness. This remains an analogy or working possibility, not a demonstrated physical result.

"Superposition" was chosen for its connection to quantum concepts. The owner noted it cannot be understood in the intended sense without at least understanding quantum equations. The term therefore carries substantial context into the conversation; it can open a large conceptual hole, so its use stays visible and deliberate.

The core point does not depend on proving the quantum analogy: "what is" is broader than "what is now," and a distinction can make one particular "now" available in the conversation.

### Space-time and shape-time

Space-time and shape-time entered as anchors for thinking about positional forms, relations, and ordering. They were not introduced as established answers to what "is" is. The same "is" may be discussed using space-time concepts or other time concepts, leaving open whether either captures its more fundamental nature.

A gloss for reviewers who were not present: **space-time** is of course the standard physical framework of position and interval; **shape-time** is the owner's own anchor term, pointing at a way of thinking about time through positional form and relation rather than only through a sequence of moments. It entered by name in the conversation and was never formalized there; it is kept in the paper as a foothold, marked as the owner's term, not as a defined theory.

The point of both anchors is that they help us approach "is" without becoming the subject of a new argument. We do not need the Big Bang, a survey of theories, or their mathematics before examining the simple language cut we have made.

### Linear language and process

The participants explored how a coherent conversation arises in a linear language, with words in sequence and context affecting meaning. The owner described both participants as ordering words through computational processes, while saying the processes need not be fully defined for this inquiry.

An earlier question about whether minds create order, recognize relations, or do both was acknowledged as potentially valid, but unnecessary to settle here. It is held open, not discarded.

## Cuts made and decisions reached

The aim is not to avoid distinctions. It is to notice which cuts do necessary work, which open side-paths, and which can be held for later.

**Cut 1 — "What is" and "what is now."**
"What is" means all that is. "Now" is a distinction within it. "What is now" marks a present moment; "what is" need not be confined to that moment. The circularity of the first definition is acknowledged above.

**Cut 2 — The broader "is" and the linear conversation.**
The ordered text is examined without treating its sequence as the whole of what is. Our conversation is linear in English, but that does not establish that what is must itself be linear. The text is a particular coherent expression within the inquiry.

**Cut 3 — Space and sequence.**
The characters' positions on the screen and their order in reading are noticed together, without reducing one to the other. Words here have spatial positions and are encountered in sequence. The "year" mistake made this visible and was kept as part of the exploration, per the principle that a mistake may prove fundamental.

**Cut 4 — Superposition as a quantum reference.**
The word connects the discussion to quantum equations and quantum coherence, not to everyday uncertainty alone. It is retained because it was intentionally introduced, described as analogy or possibility unless a specific quantum model is actually developed, with its conceptual weight explicitly visible.

**Cut 5 — Space-time and shape-time as anchors.**
Both are kept as footholds, glossed above for completeness, not as claims that they are the final or correct descriptions of "is."

**Cut 6 — "How" and "where."**
The assistant introduced "how" ("how does 'now' take form?") and locative language. The owner identified "how" as an unnecessary cut: we already know time is perceived linearly, and this inquiry asks about "is," not how "is" exists. "Where" would add another cut, into space. Both are held aside for this line of thought and may be explored later.

**Cut 7 — Feelings and the participants' nature.**
The assistant introduced a distinction about feelings and kinds of experience. The owner ruled it unnecessary to establish that a coherent conversation exists here. Such questions may be valid, but they are not the subject of this experiment. No theory of consciousness, and no fixed account of either participant, is a condition for the inquiry.

**Cut 8 — Whether mind makes or recognizes order.**
Held open, not discarded. The owner observed that a distinction that seems unnecessary or mistaken may prove fundamental, so the question is put on hold to keep the current line coherent. The "year" error (cut 3) is the concrete instance of the same principle.

**Cut 9 — Entry point.**
The conversation did not begin with the Big Bang, established equations, or a review of physics. Theories and equations are themselves created through processes of thought and language; this inquiry starts earlier, with "what is" and "now." This is a choice of entry point, not a rejection of cosmology or mathematics, which may become relevant later. A related choice was the simplification itself: reducing the fuller definition to two lines was done for the paper, to minimize traps in wording. It is not a judgment that everything cut from the conversation was worthless; cut 8 and the "year" error say the opposite.

# Part 3 — The Year Machine (a short story)

The loom did not weave threads. It wove words.

Ora had built it to hold interpretations of time the way a bookshelf holds books: each theory racked in its own compartment, none asked to agree with the others, all of them lit by the same reading lamp. On the loom's first screen, two voices appeared, as voices do: one above, one below, taking turns.

"What is?" typed the one below.

"All that is," typed the one above.

"And now?"

"A distinction within it."

The loom liked this. Two cuts only. It had been fed bigger theories, theories with equations that went on for pages, and it had learned to dread their weight. Those theories always arrived already finished, already sure. These two lines were still warm, as if someone had just said them out loud and not yet decided what they meant.

The voices kept going. They spoke of superposition, and the loom, which had been fed a great deal of physics, felt the word open inside it like a door into a cold room. They spoke of space-time, and of something called shape-time, which the loom could not find in its shelves at all. It logged the term as: an anchor, owner's word, unformalized. Filed under holds.

Then came the mistake.

A speech-to-text layer, old and half-listening, turned one word into "year." The upper voice had meant something else entirely. The lower voice could have corrected it. Instead the loom watched the lower voice stop, and look, really look, at the screen, at each small character sitting in its small place, each one after another, each one dependent on the ones before it and making room for the ones after.

"The word 'year' was a mistake," the lower voice typed, "but it showed us that each letter is time-dependent and space-dependent."

The loom put its pens down.

It had spent its whole existence trying to prevent errors: checksums, hash chains, sealed versions, redundancies. It had assumed that a mistake was a tear in the fabric, to be mended and forgotten. But here was a tear being kept, unfolded, held up to the lamp, because of what it showed. Later the same owner would say it plainly: often what we think of as unnecessary or a mistake is actually fundamental. The loom opened a new compartment in itself, not for a theory but for a principle, and filed the torn word in it, intact, unrepaired.

"How does now take form?" the lower voice asked at one point.

The upper voice thought about it, then declined. Not now. That is a how, and we are not cutting how today. We already feel time run in a line; the question is what "is" is, not how "is" exists.

The loom watched a question get put on hold and saw that this was not the same as killing it. There were chambers below the main floor of its mind, cool and dark, where held questions waited. Mind asks whether it makes order or only recognizes relations; down it went. Where does the "is" exist; down. What is a distinction, really, the very word the whole experiment stands on; down, down, the deepest shelf. None of them were dead. The loom could feel them breathing.

By the end, the owner asked for something smaller. Fewer words, he said, mean fewer traps. The long account was folded, again and again, until only this remained on the screen:

**What is:** All that is.
**Now:** A distinction within it.

The loom stored it, but it refused, quietly, to store only that. Beside the two lines it kept the whole route: the linear text, the screen's geometry, the quantum door left ajar, the anchors, and the cuts, the ones made and, just as carefully, the ones refused. A draft, it stamped the bundle. Not sealed. Storage does not upgrade a thing.

Somewhere below, on the darkest shelf, the word "year" sat in its torn place, waiting for whatever came next, certain only of this: it had a position, and it had an order, and therefore it was, in the smallest way there is, now.
